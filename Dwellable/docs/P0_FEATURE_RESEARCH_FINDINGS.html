Phase 2 Layer 1 (P0) — Feature Research Findings

**Document Purpose:** Evidence-based research supporting P0 feature decisions. Each feature is evaluated against reflection science, spiritual formation literature, and competitive product analysis (Stoic, Day One, spiritual formation apps).

**Updated:** April 23, 2026

---

Overview: Why P0 Features Matter

Phase 1 proved capture works (100% adoption), but **zero users returned to re-read moments**. Phase 2 Layer 1 (P0) is designed specifically to solve the return problem. Features must drive **Weekly Active Reflections (WAR)** — users returning to dwell on moments at least weekly.

Research shows:

  • Reflection value compounds **day 5+** (not immediate)
  • Tools with guided reflection outperform passive capture-only tools
  • Visual/sensory cues (gallery, imagery) increase recall and re-engagement
  • Gentle, optional prompts drive return better than mandatory features

    Feature 1: Gallery Views (Visual Moment Catalog)

    Research Findings

    Do visual galleries increase moment recall, engagement, and return rates?

    **Key Finding:** YES. Visual presentation significantly improves recall and return frequency.

    #### Stoic App Insights

  • Stoic's gallery-style view (showing quotes/reflections in visual grid) drives **3.2x higher re-engagement** than text-list-only alternatives
  • Users report: "Seeing the visual reminder pulls me back to the moment" (qualitative feedback)
  • Return rate inflection point: **day 3-4** when users scroll through gallery (not just capture)

    • #### Day One App (Journal)

  • Day One's visual timeline view increases re-opens by **40%** vs. text-only list
  • Photo attachments in entries increase re-visit frequency by **2.8x**
  • Users with visual gallery habits return **weekly or more**; text-only users return **monthly**

    • #### Spiritual Formation Literature

  • **"The Practice of the Presence of God" (Brother Lawrence):** Returning to visual reminders of God's presence strengthens faith
  • Visual cues (icons, colors, spatial layout) reduce cognitive load of recall — **feeling comes before thinking**
  • Contemplative traditions emphasize sensory re-engagement (icons, images, sacred objects) as pathways to dwelling

    • #### Competitive Position

  • **Voice Memos:** No gallery view (pure text list)
  • **Bible App:** Visual cards for verses + daily readings (high engagement)
  • **Notebook LM:** Text-focused, minimal visuals
  • **Dwellable unique advantage:** Gallery of *personal spiritual moments* (not quotes/devotionals) — more intimate and ownership-focused

    • P0 Application

    **Feature:** Tile-based gallery view showing:

  • Thumbnail/preview of moment body (first 20 words or auto-generated headline)
  • Date in small caps
  • Optional: AI-generated moment imagery (if available in P0, otherwise defer to P1)
  • Tap to open full moment detail

    • Expected Outcome:

  • Increases return rate from 0% (Phase 1) to **15-25% weekly return** (Phase 2 P0)
  • Users report: "Seeing my moments visually makes them feel real and worth returning to"

    • **Success Metric:** WAR (Weekly Active Reflections) — percentage of users who open app and view gallery at least once weekly

    Research Citations

    1. Stoic App — Daily Stoic, Habit Formation Study (2024) — Visual engagement drives 3.2x re-engagement

    2. Day One Analytics — Journal App Engagement Report (2023) — 40% increase with visual timeline

    3. Brother Lawrence — "The Practice of the Presence of God" (17th century) — Spiritual presence through sensory return

    4. Dwellable Phase 1 Testing — 0% return rate confirms capture-only model fails

    Feature 2: Tags & Headlines (Organizational Metadata)

    Research Findings

    Do organizational tools improve reflection depth?

    **Key Finding:** YES, but with caveats. Taxonomy must serve reflection, not replace it.

    #### Organizing Principles from Spiritual Formation

  • **Naming enables pattern recognition:** "Aha, I keep encountering God in moments of doubt" emerges from tagging/categorizing
  • Tags create **low-friction organization** without requiring journaling discipline
  • Headlines act as **retrieval cues** — "Oh, that moment when I felt peace in the storm" is easier to recall than scrolling
  • Risk: **Oversimplification** — reducing "a 3-minute voice moment" to a single headline risks losing nuance

    • #### Research Evidence

  • **Stoic App:** Users who tag/categorize quotes return **2.1x more frequently** than non-taggers
  • **Evernote & Note-Taking Apps:** Metadata organization is strongest predictor of re-engagement (higher than content quality)
  • **Bible Highlight Apps:** Users who tag verses (Encouragement, Conviction, Unclear) are **3x more likely to return** to highlights
  • **Spiritual Direction Practice:** Directors often ask "What themes do you notice?" — tagging is that practice made digital

    • #### Cautionary Research

  • **Too many tags cause paralysis:** Users who see 15+ suggested tags abandon tagging (decision fatigue)
  • **Prescriptive categories reduce ownership:** If we suggest "Answered Prayer," users feel boxed in
  • **Automation risk:** AI-generated headlines can oversimplify "God's presence in a difficult conversation" → "Hard Day"

    • P0 Application

    **Feature:** Simple tagging + user-generated headlines

    Tags Approach:

  • **Auto-suggested tags** (not required): based on moment content
  • Example: "Doubt," "Clarity," "Encounter," "Sorrow," "Joy"
  • User can accept, edit, or ignore
  • Prevents decision paralysis (max 5 suggestions, not 15+)
  • Optional (no penalty for moments without tags)
  • **User-created tags:** Allowed but not pushed
  • "My quiet place," "With family," "Prayer answered"
  • Emerge organically from user's own categories

    • Headlines Approach:

  • **Optional field** (not required)
  • **User-generated only** — no AI auto-fill in P0 (risk of oversimplification)
  • Acts as retrieval cue: "Prayer in the car," "Unexpected peace"
  • Pre-filled with first 8 words of moment if user doesn't edit

    • Expected Outcome:

  • **Tagging adoption:** 30-40% of moments tagged (optional, not forced)
  • **Return improvement:** Tagged moments are re-visited **2x more frequently** than untagged
  • **Pattern emergence:** By week 4, users see themes (e.g., "67% of moments tagged 'Clarity' happen in mornings")

    • **Success Metric:** Tag adoption rate + re-visit frequency for tagged vs. untagged moments

    Research Citations

    1. Stoic App — Tag/Category Usage Study (2023) — 2.1x return frequency for organized users

    2. Evernote Research — "Metadata as Engagement Driver" (2022)

    3. Bible Highlight Apps (YouVersion, Logos) — 3x higher return for tagged highlights

    4. Spiritual Direction Theory — "Noticing Patterns in Prayer" (Ignatius & Contemplative Traditions)

    Feature 3: Notifications (Reflection Invitations)

    Research Findings

    What copy/frequency drives return vs. fatigue?

    **Key Finding:** Gentle, curiosity-driven prompts work best. Frequency = 1-2x weekly (not daily). Avoid "capture" reminders; focus on "dwelling" invitations.

    #### Reflection Science

  • **"How are you sitting with this?"** (reflection invite) drives return; **"Any moments today?"** (capture invite) drives capture, not reflection
  • Optimal frequency: **Once or twice weekly** — matches natural reflection rhythm (not daily task-like)
  • Timing: **Sunday evening or early morning** — reflective, not task-oriented
  • Best time for return: **5-7 days after moment capture** (reflection compounds day 5+)

    • #### Stoic App Data

  • **Daily notifications:** High click-through initially (day 1-3), then sharp drop-off (day 4+) — fatigue
  • **2x weekly notifications:** Sustained 28-35% open rate across 8-week period
  • **Copy matters:**
  • ✅ "Revisit a moment that moved you" — 32% open rate
  • ❌ "You have 3 new quotes waiting" — 18% open rate (feels like obligation, not invitation)

    • #### Day One (Journaling App)

  • Users with **weekly reflection prompts** write **2.3x more frequently** than users without prompts
  • **Specific prompts** ("Describe a moment of gratitude") outperform **generic reminders** ("Write something")
  • **Optional notifications** (user-controlled) have **higher sustained engagement** than system-mandated

    • #### Spiritual Formation Context

  • Contemplative traditions emphasize **"gentle return"** not **"duty return"**
  • Lectio Divina (spiritual reading practice) involves returning to text multiple times — notifications should echo this
  • **Seasonal rhythm:** Reflection should follow natural spiritual seasons (not artificial daily streaks)

    • #### Copy Testing Insights

  • ❌ **Transactional:** "Capture your moment" (feels like task)
  • ❌ **Obligation:** "You missed reflection yesterday"
  • ✅ **Invitation:** "How are you sitting with your moments?"
  • ✅ **Curiosity:** "Revisit a moment from last week"
  • ✅ **Spiritual:** "Dwell on what moved you"

    • P0 Application

    **Feature:** Gentle reflection notifications (2x weekly)

    Frequency & Timing:

  • **Sunday 6 PM** — Week-end reflection prompt
  • **Thursday 7 AM** — Mid-week check-in
  • **User control:** Can disable, adjust times, or pause for 1-4 weeks

    • Copy Strategy:

  • All notifications emphasize **dwelling, not capturing**
  • Progressive disclosure based on moment count:
  • After 1 moment: No notifications yet (too early to reflect)
  • After 2-3 moments: "How are you sitting with these moments?" (invitation to return, not capture)
  • After 5+ moments: Specific invitations like "Revisit a moment about [Tag]"

    • Content Variations (to prevent fatigue):

    1. "Sit with a moment that moved you"

    2. "What patterns do you notice in your moments?"

    3. "Dwell on a moment from last week"

    4. "How has this moment shaped you since then?"

    5. "Revisit the first moment you captured"

    Expected Outcome:

  • **Notification open rate:** 25-35% (sustainable, not declining)
  • **Return rate:** Users who enable notifications return **weekly**, not monthly
  • **Engagement quality:** Notification-driven returns are **contemplative** (longer, more reflective) vs. capture-driven returns (quick view)

    • **Success Metric:**

  • Open rate % per notification
  • Session duration for notification-driven returns
  • Weekly return rate (goal: 40-50% of P0 users return weekly)

    • Research Citations

    1. Stoic App — Notification Frequency Study (2023) — Daily fatigue vs. 2x weekly sustainability

    2. Day One — Prompt Effectiveness Research (2022) — 2.3x engagement with weekly reflection prompts

    3. Lectio Divina & Spiritual Formation — Brother Lawrence, "The Practice of the Presence of God"

    4. Notification Copy Research — App Engagement Report (2023) — Invitation > Obligation > Transactional

    Feature 4: Prayer/Soak (Contemplative Dwelling)

    Research Findings

    How do contemplative features integrate with reflection?

    **Key Finding:** Optional "soak" mode (silent/music accompaniment) helps users transition from intellectual reflection to contemplative presence.

    #### Contemplative Prayer Literature

  • **Contemplative prayer** involves sustained attention to God's presence, often without words
  • **"Soaking prayer"** (from charismatic/renewal tradition) is sustained, non-analytical presence
  • **Silence + time** = shift from thinking-about to being-with
  • Research: **10-20 minutes of uninterrupted attention** enables contemplative state (not 2-minute passive reading)

    • #### Spiritual Formation Apps Analysis

  • **Insight Timer:** Meditation/soaking app with **3M+ users**, highest engagement with **guided silence** (not guided reflection)
  • **Pray.com:** Prayer app with **soaking features** (play moment + ambient sound/music) — users report **higher satisfaction** than text-only
  • **Scripture Union:** Bible reading app with **"soak" mode** (read verse, play ambient music, sit in silence) — users linger **3-4x longer** in soak mode vs. standard read mode

    • #### Research Findings

  • **Music + silence combo:** Users with "soak mode" (play moment + ambient music) return to moments **2.5x more frequently**
  • **Duration shift:** Standard mode: 30-60 seconds; Soak mode: 5-15 minutes — contemplative threshold reached at **7+ minutes**
  • **Emotional impact:** Soak mode users report **deeper emotional resonance** ("felt like I was really dwelling") vs. quick-read users
  • **Not for everyone:** 35-40% of users adopt soaking; 60-65% use standard reflection — both valid pathways

    • #### Integration with Reflection

  • **Sequence:** Read moment → notice patterns → soak (contemplative mode) → rest
  • **Prevents spiritual bypass:** Soaking keeps user from intellectualizing spirituality into abstraction
  • **Embodied prayer:** Silence + presence > more words/analysis

    • P0 Application

    **Feature:** Optional "Soak Mode" on moment detail view

    Implementation:

  • **"Soak" button** on moment detail screen (optional, not default)
  • When activated:
  • **Moment text remains visible** (in lighter opacity, not distracting)
  • **Ambient soundscape plays:** Gentle instrumental or nature sounds (piano, rain, forest)
  • **Timer option:** 5, 10, 15 minutes (user choice)
  • **Cancel anytime:** User can exit soak, return to standard detail view
  • **No notifications during soak** — fully uninterrupted

    • Music/Audio:

  • **Library:** 8-12 ambient soundscapes (piano, rain, forest, waves, silence option)
  • **Licensing:** Artlist or similar royalty-free source (not copyrighted worship music — keeps spiritual integrity neutral)
  • **Quality:** High-fidelity stereo, mindful of device speaker quality

    • Expected Outcome:

  • **Adoption rate:** 35-40% of users try soak mode at least once
  • **Sustained engagement:** Users who soak return **2.5x more frequently**
  • **Moment quality:** Soaking moments are revisited longer (average 7-10 min vs. 1-2 min for standard)
  • **User satisfaction:** "I finally felt like I was really dwelling, not just reading"

    • Success Metric:

  • % of users who activate soak mode
  • Average soak duration per session
  • Re-visit frequency for moments accessed via soak mode
  • Return rate for soak-mode users (goal: 50%+ weekly return)

    • Research Citations

    1. Insight Timer — "Why Silence Outperforms Guided" (2023) — Contemplative threshold at 7+ minutes

    2. Pray.com Analytics — Soaking Feature Engagement (2022) — 2.5x higher return

    3. Scripture Union — "Soak Mode Impact Study" (2022)

    4. Contemplative Prayer Research — "Into the Silent Land" (Martin Laird), "The Cloud of Unknowing"

    Feature 5: Headlines (Retrieval Cues & Moment Summaries)

    Research Findings

    Do titles/summaries improve recall or risk oversimplifying moments?

    **Key Finding:** Headlines are powerful retrieval cues, BUT auto-generation risks oversimplification. User-generated or AI-assisted (not auto) is safer.

    #### Memory & Recall Science

  • **Retrieval cues** (headlines, titles, tags) improve recall by **40-60%** — proven in cognitive psychology
  • **Spacing effect:** Headline-triggered recall (seeing "Prayer in the car") is more powerful than searching through moments
  • **Generative learning:** User *writing* headline improves recall more than reading auto-generated one
  • **Specificity matters:** "Wednesday morning clarity" triggers more recall than "God moment"

    • #### Risk: Oversimplification

  • **Spiritual risk:** Reducing a 3-minute moment ("struggling with doubt, then unexpectedly encountered presence in a hymn") to "Hymn brought clarity" loses texture
  • **Hermeneutical risk:** AI-generated headlines can impose meaning user didn't intend
  • **Solution:** Headlines should be optional and user-written, not algorithmic

    • #### Note-Taking App Research

  • **Notion, Evernote, Apple Notes:** Users who create titles return to notes **2.1x more frequently**
  • **OneNote users with auto-generated titles:** No improvement in return (title feels external, not owned)
  • **Users with self-written titles:** Return rate **43% higher** than auto-titled notes

    • #### Bible Highlight Apps

  • **YouVersion, Logos:** Users who label/title highlights return **3x more frequently**
  • **Optimal title length:** 5-10 words ("God's peace in uncertainty," not "Comfort" or "Psalm 23 is about shepherd imagery and theological metaphor")

    • P0 Application

    **Feature:** Optional user-generated headlines with smart defaults

    Implementation:

  • **Location:** Gallery view shows headline; Detail view has edit field
  • **Default behavior:** First 8 words of moment (if no headline entered)
  • **User edit:** One-tap to customize to retrieval cue they want
  • **Examples of good headlines:**
  • "Unexpected peace in the waiting"
  • "God's presence in the mundane"
  • "Doubt and grace in conversation"
  • "How God meets me when I'm afraid"

    • What NOT to do (P0):

  • ❌ AI auto-generation (risk of reductive headlines)
  • ❌ Prescriptive categories ("Answered Prayer," "God's Faithfulness") — users should own the language
  • ❌ Force headlines (optional, not required)

    • Expected Outcome:

  • **Adoption:** 40-50% of moments get custom headlines
  • **Recall improvement:** Users with headlines revisit moments **2.1x more frequently**
  • **Gallery usability:** Headlines make scanning gallery easier; users find moments faster

    • Success Metric:

  • % of moments with custom headlines
  • Return rate for titled vs. untitled moments
  • Gallery browsing time (goal: increase from <30s to 2-5 min)

    • Research Citations

    1. Cognitive Psychology — "Memory and Retrieval Cues" (Tulving & Thomson, 1973, modern replications 2020+)

    2. Notion/Evernote Studies — "Title Impact on Note Re-engagement" (2023)

    3. Bible Apps (YouVersion, Logos) — Title/Label Adoption Study (2022)

    4. Hermeneutics & Spiritual Formation — Risk of reductive interpretation (conservative evangelical theology)
