The Script

This is the narration that the animation should visualize. There is no text in the animation itself — these words are the emotional throughline, not on-screen copy. The designer should read this and ask: what does this feel like as moving images?

Every moment with God is precious. The way you determine it to be precious is based on how well you steward it.

Take, for example, this: imagine you're on a walk, and the Lord whispers to you, "You're my child, and I love you very much." Afterward, a wave of peace overwhelms you. What would you do after that? You might forget about it.

Dwelly helps to ensure that you never forget your God moments.

Whether it's an angel, a prayer answered, a dream you had that needs to be written down so you never forget it, or even a Bible verse that's very challenging — Dwelly will enable you to process every encounter you have with God, no matter how big or small. Afterward, it will create a journal that reflects everything that you felt, heard, and thought in that moment, so you never forget it.

After you do this for so long, you have an entire history of God moments that you can always recall whenever you are discouraged or even happy.

The animation does not need to illustrate every sentence literally. It needs to carry the emotional arc: stillness → intimacy → risk of loss → capture → abundance.

Scene Breakdown

Scene 1 The Precious Moment — Awakening 0 – 8s
Script line "Every moment with God is precious."
Visual Pure darkness. A single warm light source emerges from the very center of the frame — slowly, gently, like the first light of dawn or a single candle being lit. Small gold particles begin to drift upward from the light source.
Motion Fade in from black. Light expands outward. Particles rise slowly — unhurried, weightless. No sharp movements.
Mood Sacred Still Anticipatory
Scene 2 The Walk — A Divine Whisper 8 – 18s
Script line "Imagine you're on a walk, and the Lord whispers to you, 'You're my child, and I love you very much.' A wave of peace overwhelms you."
Visual A gentle implied movement — like drifting slowly through soft light and shadow. The light source breathes and expands. A golden warmth radiates outward like a whisper made visible — particles swirl gently around the center, then envelop the frame in a soft wash of warmth.
Motion Slow lateral drift (no hard camera moves). At the "wave of peace" moment (~13s), the light blooms — a burst of warmth that fills the screen before softening back. Particles cluster and feel like an embrace.
Mood Intimate Warm Peaceful
Scene 3 The Risk — Forgetting 18 – 24s
Script line "What would you do after that? You might forget about it."
Visual The warmth begins to drift. The particles that had gathered start to scatter outward and fade at the edges. The central light contracts slightly — not dark, not alarming, but quietly dimming. A gentle sense of loss without despair.
Motion Particles drift away from center and fade. Light source contracts. A slow, graceful dissipation — not a sudden change. The transition should feel like a memory becoming hazy, not like something bad happening.
Mood Quiet tension Wistful
Scene 4 The Capture — Dwelly 24 – 33s
Script line "Dwelly helps to ensure that you never forget your God moments."
Visual The scattered particles reverse — they are drawn back toward the center, as if collected. The light stabilizes and deepens into a warmer, fuller glow. This is the moment of preservation: a sense that something precious is being held safely.
Motion Particles flow back inward in a gathering motion — not frantic, but intentional. Light blooms back to full warmth. The central glow settles into a steady, confident pulse. Optional: a subtle circular "seal" or ring that forms around the light, suggesting a boundary of remembrance.
Mood Reassurance Safety Warmth
Scene 5 The History — A Constellation of Moments 33 – 40s
Script line "After you do this for so long, you have an entire history of God moments that you can always recall."
Visual From the central light, secondary smaller lights appear — each one a captured moment. They settle into a loose constellation surrounding the center: 5–7 smaller glowing points, each pulsing gently out of sync with each other, like a sky full of stars that are alive.
Motion Secondary lights drift in from outside the frame and settle into position, one by one. All points pulse softly together. The animation reaches its final resting state — full, warm, alive — and holds there. This is the last frame the user sees before "Continue" appears.
Mood Abundant Full Hopeful

Style Direction

What this animation is

DOAbstract — particles, light, glow. Evocative of the sacred, not literal.
DOSlow and contemplative. Every motion should feel intentional, unhurried.
DOVector-only. No raster images, no embedded photos.
DOSmooth easing on all motions — ease-in-out, nothing linear or bouncy.
DOEnd on a graceful resting state (Scene 5's constellation) that can hold indefinitely.

What this animation is not

DON'TNo text, no words, no on-screen labels of any kind.
DON'TNo characters, people, animals, or illustrated figures.
DON'TNo religious iconography (no cross, no dove, no explicit church imagery). The spiritual feeling comes from light, warmth, and stillness — not symbols.
DON'TNo playback controls or progress bar baked into the animation itself.
DON'TNo fast movements, no bounces, no energetic transitions.
DON'TNo looping — this plays once start to finish, then holds at the final frame.

Reference feel

Think of the opening of a candle in a dark room, filmed in slow motion. Or the Opal app's onboarding video (cave with a glowing orb). Or the visual language of a starfield that is alive and breathing. The tone is meditative, not dramatic.

Technical Specifications

FormatLottie JSON (.json). Not MP4, not GIF.
Canvas size390 × 844 pt. Standard iPhone canvas. Lottie scales, so this is the master size.
Frame rate60 fps
DurationExactly 40 seconds (2400 frames at 60fps)
BackgroundTransparent or #0E0E12. Do not rely on the canvas background for color — the app sets it.
File sizeTarget under 500 KB. Avoid raster embeds, trim path complexity where possible.
Exports needed1× Lottie JSON (production) + 1× MP4 preview at 1x for client review
ToolAfter Effects with Bodymovin plugin (preferred), or Rive (Lottie-compatible export)
GradientsRadial and linear gradients are fine. Avoid mesh gradients (not Lottie-compatible).
Blur effectsPermitted sparingly — they increase file size. Keep layers with blur minimal.
Pre-compsFlatten nested pre-comps before Bodymovin export where possible for cleaner JSON.
